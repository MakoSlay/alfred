# Photo Edit Feedback

## Top Priorities

### 1. Use local adjustments for the face
The face should be the first place the viewer lands, but right now the bouquet and red door pull attention first.

**Why:** the face is sitting in slightly darker light.

**Add a local adjustment / radial mask over the:**
- forehead
- cheeks
- eyes

**Adjustments:**
- Exposure: **+0.20 to +0.35 EV**
- Shadows: **+10**
- Slight local contrast increase
- Tiny chroma boost
- Texture: **+5**

> Keep this local to the face only. Do not brighten the whole image.

### 2. Fine-tune the green channel
The greens are beautiful, but they are a little dominant.

If you're using **RawTherapee**, try the **HSV Equalizer** rather than only lowering saturation.

**Try:**
- Green Saturation: **-5% to -8%**
- Green Lightness/Luminance: **slightly up**

This keeps the ivy lush while making it feel less heavy and helps the skin separate more naturally.

### 3. Recover highlights in the dress
The whites are close to losing detail, but there is probably more texture available in the RAW.

**Try:**
- Highlights: **-15 to -20**
- Whites: **-5**
- Use RawTherapee's **highlight reconstruction** if needed

Goal: recover embroidery/fabric detail without making the dress look dull.

### 4. Let the bouquet pop a little more
The bouquet feels slightly darker than expected.

**Selective adjustment on the roses only:**
- Red Luminance: **+5**
- or Exposure: **+0.15**

### 5. Lift the deepest blacks very slightly
Some of the darkest ivy patches are nearing solid black.

**Optional:**
- Blacks: **+3 to +5**

This is a very subtle tweak to preserve foliage texture.

### 6. Check sharpening in the foliage
The image does **not** look oversharpened, which is good.

If you used **RL Deconvolution** or **Capture Sharpening**, be careful around the ivy—dense foliage can start to look brittle or crunchy very quickly.

---

## What Stands Out
Your editing feels intentional. A lot of photographers chase sliders until something looks “cool,” but this does not feel like that. The colors are cohesive and the mood is consistent.

If I had one criticism, it is that the image still feels slightly **globally edited**. The next leap in quality usually comes from spending more time with **local adjustments**—guiding the viewer's eye with selective light rather than making everything look better at once.
